# 00 · Method and Rubrics — How This Dossier Was Built, and Why You Should Trust It

**Governing thought.** Trust nothing in this dossier on the author's say-so: every conclusion names the established framework it stands on and points to the evidence behind it — cited research, or an artifact you can open and run — and this document is the index that lets you check or attack each one.

**The situation** is a strategy dossier written by a solo founder. **The complication** is that a solo founder has no boss, no board, and no PM peer to catch a flattering lie — and in this market the *friendly* evidence lies systematically, because "understand my saves" is the kind of identity-flattering idea people endorse and never act on. **The question** a skeptical reader has, in their first sixty seconds, is: why should any conclusion here be believed? **The answer** is the method described below. It makes four promises about every conclusion in the dossier — each is built on named prior art, grounded in cited research or the already-built artifact, argued to a disputable point of view, and adversarially gated against a pre-declared rubric before it stands. This document names, for each sibling doc, the framework it uses, the rubric it was graded against, and the pointer to the evidence behind each load-bearing number. Read it first, and no conclusion in the dossier stays a black box.

That discipline is not stylistic fussiness. It is the same discipline the product's engine is designed to run on — grounding to external IDs with provenance, an eval scorecard that shows its work, terms admitted by warrant rather than whim. The method applies that discipline one level up: to the argument *about* the product, not just the product. Disagree with any conclusion in a sibling document, and this one tells you exactly which framework and which evidence to attack.

## 1. Diagnosis: why strategy documents rot, and what specifically kills them

The failure mode this method exists to prevent is well-characterized. Richard Rumelt's *Good Strategy / Bad Strategy* names four hallmarks of **bad strategy**: fluff, failure to face the challenge, mistaking goals for strategy, and bad strategic objectives ([Rumelt, kernel](https://roadmap.one/blog/posts/blog46-3-good-strategy-bad-strategy/)). A dossier written without a method drifts into all four, through three concrete channels that recur across the research corpus.

**Invented frameworks.** The most common self-deception in product strategy is to draw a bespoke 2×2, name it, and treat the naming as evidence. The engine-taxonomy research supplies the cautionary analogue: MPEG-7 failed not for lack of expressiveness but for expressiveness *without constraint*, because "an expressive vocabulary with weak constraints is worse than a modest one with strong constraints" ([Hunter, D-Lib](https://www.dlib.org/dlib/september99/hunter/09hunter.html)). Two annotators then describe the same thing differently, and agreement collapses. An invented strategic framework is the same defect — a degree of freedom nobody paid for with a definition. The information-science fix transfers directly: admit a term only by **warrant** — literary, user, or structural — never by whim ([ISKO, warrant](https://www.isko.org/cyclo/literary_warrant)). So a framework enters this dossier only if it is attested elsewhere, never because it felt clever.

**Flattering evidence.** The users-validation and JTBD research converge on a hard point: for a product in the "understand my saves" space, the friendly evidence lies. Rob Fitzpatrick's Mom Test is explicit that "opinions are worthless; the only things that matter are facts and concrete commitments" ([Mom Test](https://www.momtestbook.com/); [Rekhi](https://www.sachinrekhi.com/p/the-mom-test-rob-fitzpatrick)). This category is the worst case for interviews. "Understand my saves" is an identity-flattering aspiration; people endorse it and never act on it. A dossier that grades itself on how good its own conclusions feel is measuring the wrong thing.

**Asserted conclusions.** The competitive research shows every rival in the space "asserts results in a black box" — restaurant → map is claimed, never shown, never confidence-scored, never traceable. The same failure at the document layer is a strategy that asserts "we will win on depth" with no falsifiable definition of depth and no test that could show it doesn't. The product's answer to this is a six-axis eval that grounds and shows its work; the dossier's answer is structurally the same — a conclusion carries its evidence and its kill-condition, or it does not count.

The bounded challenge, in three parts. First, the stakes: this dossier must produce decisions a solo founder can bet 10–20 hrs/week and under \$50/mo on. Second, the trap: in this market the friendly evidence is systematically misleading, and the honest evidence is expensive to collect. Third, the consequence: the method must therefore *manufacture* trustworthiness rather than assume it.

## 2. Guiding policy: grounded, argued, adversarially gated

Three commitments narrow every choice in this dossier, in priority order. When they conflict, grounding beats eloquence, and gating beats grounding.

**(a) Grounded — no invented frameworks, ever.** Every analytical lens is named prior art with a citation. Every material claim traces to one of exactly three sources: the cited research corpus, an established external framework, or the *built artifact* — the engine and its tests, the captured corpus, and the prior enriched outputs. "Grounded" here has teeth only if each artifact claim carries a resolvable pointer, so this section is backed by an explicit ledger (below) rather than by adjectives.

The single most important honesty move is to distinguish what is *materialized and checkable today* from what is *specified but not yet built*. The engine, its 54 passing tests, the ~1,313-item captured corpus, the proven capture spike, and the prior enriched outputs all exist and can be opened or run right now. The version-locked golden set and the six-axis eval scorecard **do not yet exist** — they are designed in a sibling brief and slated to be stood up. Claiming otherwise would fail the very Provenance gate this method installs (§4). The ledger states each status plainly:

The grounding commitment has a second consequence for numbers borrowed from research. Some corpus figures are flagged directional-only — the "87% never return" figure, the "\$50–300" B2B band, the survey WTP estimates. The dossier treats each as an upper bound or a direction, never a point estimate. The same applies to the judge-evaluation figures above: the 33–41pp inflation and the kappa/swap-debias protocol are how the *product's eval is designed to behave*, drawn from the methodology literature — they are not results Attic has produced, because the eval has not been run.

**(b) Argued — a memo with a spine, not a requirements dump.** Each document follows Rumelt's kernel — **diagnosis → guiding policy → coherent action** — and Cagan's insistence on separating vision from strategy and picking battles ([Cagan/SVPG](https://www.svpg.com/vision-vs-strategy/)). The test is falsifiability of the prose itself: a reader must be able to disagree with the thesis sentence. A document no reasonable person could argue with is fluff by Rumelt's definition. So-what runs throughout, and a fact that changes no decision is cut.

**(c) Adversarially gated — every conclusion survives a hostile check before it stands.** This is the commitment that makes the other two honest, and it is lifted from the product's own validation logic. Eric Ries' Build–Measure–Learn requires each bet to end in an explicit **pivot-or-persevere** decision against a *pre-declared* threshold, so a beloved artifact cannot retroactively rationalize weak signals ([Lean Startup](https://theleanstartup.com/book)). The users-validation research turns this into concrete kill/persevere numbers: ≥~70% retrieval task success with lower time-to-find than the native baseline; Sean Ellis ≥40% "very disappointed"; at least one commitment (pay/refer/retain) per satisfied user; and, for agencies, one signed LOI or paid pilot after a concierge delivery. The dossier adopts the same stance one level up — a strategic claim is gated by engaging its *strongest* counter-evidence, not its friendliest. That is why the JTBD document leads with "this is a vitamin the market has repeatedly refused to take" *before* arguing the painkiller pockets, and why the competitive document opens by conceding that Stasht already ships the literal v1 feature list for free.

## 3. Every lens is named prior art tied to one document — no bespoke 2×2s

Because the grounding commitment forbids invented frameworks, the full set of lenses is enumerable, and each is tied to a specific document. This is the dossier's bill of materials, made public so the method is inspectable — the open-core posture applied to the argument itself.

- **Product Strategy Memo (Doc 1):** Rumelt's kernel and nine sources of power; Cagan/SVPG vision-vs-strategy and the product operating model ([Rumelt](https://roadmap.one/blog/posts/blog46-3-good-strategy-bad-strategy/); [SVPG](https://www.svpg.com/vision-vs-strategy/)).
- **PRD (Doc 2):** Amazon Working Backwards PR-FAQ (headline → FAQ, four internal-FAQ categories) fused with the Lenny Rachitsky / SVPG PRD spine ([Working Backwards](https://workingbackwards.com/resources/working-backwards-pr-faq/); [Lenny](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/prds-1-pagers-examples)).
- **Market & Competitive Analysis (Doc 3):** PESTLE (Aguilar); TAM/SAM/SOM top-down *and* bottom-up, reconciled; Porter's Five Forces; Ulwick's Outcome-Driven Innovation / JTBD; Blue Ocean's Strategy Canvas + ERRC grid ([Porter, HBR](https://hbr.org/2008/01/the-five-competitive-forces-that-shape-strategy); [Ulwick](https://anthonyulwick.com/jobs-to-be-done/); [Blue Ocean](https://www.blueoceanstrategy.com/tools/errc-grid/)).
- **Technical Design Document (Doc 4):** Google design-doc conventions (context/scope, goals & non-goals, trade-offs, alternatives considered, cross-cutting concerns), the IETF RFC review stance, and Nygard ADRs for each significant decision ([Google design docs](https://www.industrialempathy.com/posts/design-docs-at-google/); [Nygard ADR](https://www.cognitect.com/blog/2011/11/15/documenting-architecture-decisions)). This is where the engine-taxonomy and engine-platform-model research land: the durable content model (an Activity-Streams/schema.org-shaped base object with per-platform adapters), the controlled-core-plus-open-tag taxonomy, and the never-delete/warrant governance are all ADR-bearing decisions.
- **Business Model & Pricing (Doc 5):** Osterwalder's Business Model Canvas plus the operationalized value-based-pricing stack — choice-based conjoint, Van Westendorp PSM, and in-market revealed-preference testing, with survey numbers treated as upper bounds ([Strategyzer](https://www.strategyzer.com/library/the-business-model-canvas); [Van Westendorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Westendorp%27s_Price_Sensitivity_Meter)).
- **Go-To-Market (Doc 6):** the 7-step B2B GTM framework (ICP → positioning → channels → offer → enablement → launch → measurement) with primary-motion-first channel choice.

The validation methods are canon too, and they gate Docs 1–6 rather than living in any one of them: Lean Startup Build–Measure–Learn, the Mom Test, NN/g's 5-user usability finding, Sean Ellis' 40% test, Savoia's Fake-Door/Facade pretotyping, and the concierge-vs-Wizard-of-Oz distinction (Food on the Table, Zappos, Vanta/Ramp manual reports).

## 4. A seven-axis rubric with two hard gates rejected any doc that asserts rather than grounds

Every document was scored on the same seven-axis rubric before shipping. The rubric deliberately mirrors the product's own **six-axis eval design**. A founder who preaches measurable, provenance-bearing evaluation of his engine, then argues his strategy by assertion, has already failed his own thesis. Each axis names the failure it catches and the prior art it derives from. Scoring is `fail / partial / pass`; **Provenance and Diagnosis are gates** — a document that fails either is rejected regardless of the other five.

1. **Provenance (gate).** Is every framework named/established and every material claim traceable to the corpus, established prior art, or the built artifact — with materialized-vs-specified stated honestly? *Fails on:* an invented framework, an asserted number with no source, or a claim that an unbuilt artifact exists. *Derives from:* warrant discipline (ISKO), MPEG-7's "constrain every degree of freedom," and the product's grounding-with-provenance principle.
2. **Diagnosis-first (gate).** Does it name a specific, bounded problem before prescribing anything? *Fails on:* an environmental scan, or goals dressed as strategy. *Derives from:* Rumelt's kernel and four hallmarks.
3. **Committed POV.** Is there a thesis sentence a reasonable reader could dispute, with so-what carried throughout? *Fails on:* a neutral requirements dump. *Derives from:* Cagan "pick your battles"; Rumelt "focus."
4. **Coherence.** Do the actions and claims reinforce each other and stay consistent across sibling documents (Doc 3's consumer-as-funnel logic matching Doc 5's pricing logic matching Doc 6's ICP)? *Fails on:* a laundry list of unrelated initiatives. *Derives from:* Rumelt's coherent action.
5. **Falsifiability & measurement.** Are claims tied to pre-declared metrics or kill/persevere thresholds, with hypothesis distinguished from fact? *Fails on:* an unfalsifiable promise ("users will love it"). *Derives from:* Ries' pivot-or-persevere, Sean Ellis 40%, the six-axis eval design, and Ulwick's importance-vs-satisfaction scoring.
6. **Adversarial gating.** Did the conclusion engage its *strongest* counter-evidence, and are judgment calls validated rather than self-graded? *Fails on:* citing only confirming evidence; trusting an unvalidated judge. *Derives from:* the Mom Test ("opinions are worthless"), VidFactScore claim-level faithfulness, and the kappa-validated / swap-debiased LLM-judge protocol the eval is designed around.
7. **Durability discipline.** Does it separate durable from transient — applying the competitive "can a rival copy this in <2 years?" bar and the JTBD vitamin-vs-painkiller test? *Fails on:* staking positioning on a commodity (auto-tagging, breadth, "AI"). *Derives from:* the competitive moat bar, JTBD forces-of-progress, and the taxonomy's durable-vs-adaptable tension.

The gates exist to reject, not to decorate. Axis 7 forces the consumer wedge to be labeled a proof-and-funnel loss-leader rather than a profit center. Axis 6 forces the "understand my saves" pitch to be conceded a vitamin before any optimism is allowed. Axis 5 puts the agency tier behind an LOI gate rather than an assumption. If none of the seven axes could ever change a conclusion, the rubric would be theater; these three did.

## 5. Each sibling doc uses a fixed framework-derived structure so no author can skip the embarrassing section

The outlines live in full inside each document. What matters here is why each structure *is* the argument.

- **Doc 1 (Strategy Memo):** diagnosis → vision (2–5 yr) → guiding policy → coherent action → source of power → non-goals → OKRs → bad-strategy self-check. The non-goals and self-check sections force axes 3 and 2. You cannot pass without stating what you refuse to do and auditing yourself against Rumelt's four hallmarks.
- **Doc 2 (PRD):** the Amazon PR-FAQ (customer-first, ≤2 pages) front-loads the customer benefit before any feature list — a structural guard against the asserted-solution failure. Its four-category internal FAQ (customer/TAM, problems/assumptions, economics, dependencies + metrics) is where axis 5 lives.
- **Doc 3 (Market & Competitive):** PESTLE → TAM/SAM/SOM → Five Forces → JTBD/ODI → competitor teardown → Blue Ocean → synthesis. The "where we win" claim arrives *after* the industry structure and unmet-need evidence that must support it. This is where the corpus's Porter and Blue Ocean work (two markets, one white space) and the vitamin-vs-painkiller verdict land.
- **Doc 4 (Technical Design):** Google's goals/non-goals + proposed design + alternatives-considered + ADRs. Every significant choice — unified content model vs. per-platform, controlled-core taxonomy vs. free-tag, eager capture-time poster capture — records its trade-offs and its rejected alternatives. That is the engine research's "never delete, always account" governance applied to decisions.
- **Doc 5 (Business Model & Pricing):** the nine-block Canvas paired with the CBC + Van Westendorp + in-market stack. It is required to state where value-based pricing *doesn't* apply — the honesty valve for a bimodal-WTP market.
- **Doc 6 (GTM):** starts from an ICP precise enough to disqualify a lead, not a TAM, then orders positioning → channels → offer → enablement → launch → measurement behind decision gates.

Cross-references are load-bearing, not decorative (axis 4). Doc 3's white-space verdict feeds Doc 1's source of power. Doc 3's JTBD pockets constrain Doc 2's headline and Doc 6's ICP. Doc 5's WTP band and Doc 6's Dewey-style export-pass funnel must agree with Doc 1's "consumer-as-funnel, agency-as-revenue" policy. And Doc 4's eval scorecard is, once built, the sales artifact Doc 6 hands skeptical agency buyers.

## 6. Why this method, and not the alternatives — with its limits named

A method that preaches "alternatives considered, with pros and cons" (Doc 4's ADR discipline) owes the same accounting of itself. Three plausible alternatives were considered and rejected as the *primary* check; each survives, in part, inside the chosen method.

- **Ship-and-measure only** — skip the dossier, build, and A/B in market. *Pros:* fastest; real behavioral data beats any framework; avoids analysis-paralysis. *Cons:* this market's friendly signals mislead (the vitamin category), so premature shipping measures the wrong thing; a solo founder at 10–20 hrs/week cannot run enough parallel experiments to learn cheaply from raw traffic; and it forfeits the portfolio-artifact value of a reasoned dossier. *Verdict:* rejected as the sole method, but adopted as the method's *terminal* step — the §2(c) pre-registered kill/persevere gates are exactly ship-and-measure, made honest by being written down first.
- **External advisor / peer review as the check** — outsource the adversary. *Pros:* a genuine outside critic is the strongest debias and catches blind spots a rubric cannot. *Cons:* not reliably available to this founder; advisor bandwidth is scarce and slow; and it imports the advisor's own priors. *Verdict:* rejected as the *primary* check because it cannot be guaranteed to exist when a decision is due — but the open, published-method posture is designed to make external review cheap and welcome whenever it is available.
- **A lightweight one-page rubric** — a single checklist instead of seven axes with gates. *Pros:* low overhead; more likely to actually be used; avoids over-engineering. *Cons:* too coarse to catch the specific failures that kill strategy docs here — invented frameworks, flattering evidence, ungated conclusions — because a one-liner cannot gate. *Verdict:* rejected as insufficient, but its spirit is honored: only two axes (Provenance, Diagnosis) are hard gates that do the heavy lifting, while the other five stay light.

The chosen method is thus a synthesis — pre-registered ship-and-measure gates, a structured-but-gated rubric, and openness that invites outside review — and it is the right one here for three reasons specific to this founder. **First, method is the only check available:** a solo founder's confirmation bias has no counterweight except the pre-declared rubric and adversarial gate. **Second, the method is itself the portfolio artifact:** a dossier that grounds its claims, cites its canon, publishes its rubric, and reaches conclusions its author wishes were otherwise demonstrates the same rigor the engine's eval is meant to show. **Third, it is meta-consistent with the product:** the engine grounds to durable external IDs and refuses to assert what it cannot show; the dossier grounds to cited frameworks and refuses to conclude what it cannot gate. For a trust-first, show-your-work product, the method and the product make the same promise.

The limits, named so they cannot ambush a reader (axes 5 and 6 turned on the method itself):

- **(a) Grounding to prior art inherits prior art's blind spots.** Porter under-weights fast platform disruption. So the competitive analysis pairs it with Blue Ocean and treats the incumbent platforms themselves as the sharpest supplier and substitute threat.
- **(b) Cited research is only as good as its sources.** Many of the corpus's most quotable numbers are vendor-blog directional figures. The defense is to demote them to directions and gate real decisions on the founder's own forthcoming 5–10-user retrieval tests, not on borrowed statistics.
- **(c) The provenance gate is only as honest as its ledger.** Two of the artifacts this dossier leans on — the version-locked golden set and the six-axis scorecard — are specified, not built (§2a). Until they exist and produce numbers, every claim that depends on them is a design claim, not a result, and is marked as such. The method's credibility rests on keeping that line visible rather than blurring it.
- **(d) The largest residual risk is the one the users-validation research names:** that the engine's genuine craftsmanship substitutes for evidence. No rubric neutralizes that — only the pre-declared kill criteria, executed honestly, do. The method makes the founder write the numbers that would make him stop *before* he sees the results. Whether he then honors them is the one thing method cannot enforce, and naming that limit is the method's final application to itself.
